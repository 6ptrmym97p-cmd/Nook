Country guide

Where can you stay overnight?

Summary for cyclists with a small tent. Use this as a starting point; local signs, fire danger, site management and permission remain leading.

Always do

  • Check the official source before departure.
  • Ask permission on private land.
  • Avoid reserves, fields and houses.
  • No fire in times of drought or doubt.

Background by region

These guides provide general information about camping and staying overnight. They are not specific places to sleep; they are therefore not among the locations on the map.

Småland: lakes and production forest

Växjö Region · Sweden

A promising region for a discreet overnight stay under the right of public access. Find uncultivated land well away from homes and check local nature reserve rules.

Stay
1–2 nights
Cost
Free
Water
Filter water or refill elsewhere

Please check: No right to fire. In the event of drought, a complete fire ban may apply. Stay away from houses, fields and vulnerable nature.

Brandenburg Lake Region

North of Berlin · Germany

Brandenburg offers more options for non-motorized travelers than many other states, but ownership, forest rights and protected areas remain decisive.

Stay
Usually 1 night
Cost
Free with valid permission
Water
Not guaranteed

Please check: Don't automatically consider a beautiful forest plot as permission. Better choose a trekking spot or ask the owner.

Utrechtse Heuvelrug

Province of Utrecht · Netherlands

Wild camping is not generally permitted here. Host networks, private gardens and landowner permission can help you find an affordable stay along this corridor.

Stay
By appointment
Cost
Depends on the platform
Water
At host

Please check: Wild camping is not a legal standard option here. Use a guest network or request permission on private property before arriving.

Skåneleden shelters

Skåne · Sweden

There are shelters and simple rest areas along long-distance routes. Check per shelter whether reservations, making a fire or tents next to it are allowed.

Stay
Usually 1 night
Cost
Often free
Water
Not always present

Please check: A shelter marking says nothing about availability. Always take a tent with you as a fallback option.

OpenStreetMap + local route manager ↗
Norwegian utmark

Hardangervidda region · Norway

In Norwegian Utmark you can usually spend the night with a small tent. This regional guide does not describe a specific location: check locally whether the land is actually undeveloped and uncultivated.

Stay
Maximum 2 nights; longer in remote mountain areas
Cost
Free
Water
Check and treat the water yourself

Please check: Stay at least 150 meters from inhabited houses and cabins. Local rules apply, among other things, in busy areas; fire in or near forest is normally prohibited from April 15 to September 15. A DNT or other mountain hut is not automatically free.

Belgian bivouac zones

Ardennes and Flemish nature reserves · Belgium

Wild camping is not generally permitted, but Belgium has basic bivouac areas along walking and cycling routes. Find designated sites using the individual OpenStreetMap pins, then check with the land manager.

Stay
Reserve ANB zones in advance for free; elsewhere according to manager
Cost
Often free
Water
Varies

Please check: This is regional guidance, not a specific place to stay. Flanders, Wallonia and local land managers set their own conditions; nature reserves are not open camping grounds.

Agency for Nature and Forest ↗
French bivouac regions

Alps, Pyrenees and other route areas · France

France has no general right to wild camp. A brief bivouac may be permitted locally, especially along long-distance routes, but each national park, nature reserve and municipality can set its own areas and times.

Stay
Usually only from evening to morning where permitted
Cost
Free where explicitly permitted
Water
Not guaranteed

Please check: Ask permission on private land and check the park page. Coasts, protected or classified locations, roads and locally prohibited zones are excluded; Strict fire regulations apply in Mediterranean areas.

Luxembourg: permission or official place

Mullerthal and Oesling · Luxembourg

Luxembourg has no general right to wild camp. Use an official campsite or a place with explicit permission; forests, nature reserves and route verges are not open overnight sites.

Stay
By appointment or according to site rules
Cost
Varies
Water
At official location

Please check: This guide is just a warning and search start. Use OSM for marked areas or ask the owner for permission in advance.

Swiss high alpine bivouac zone

Above the tree line · Switzerland

A single, careful night above treeline is possible in many places, but Switzerland has cantonal and municipal restrictions and many strictly protected areas.

Stay
One night, set up late and leave early
Cost
Free where allowed
Water
Check and treat the water yourself

Please check: Not in nature reserves, game rest areas or the Swiss National Park. Avoid riverbanks and floodplains, stay out of sight of huts and ask permission near alpine meadows or private land.

Austria: forest is excluded

Alpine region · Austria

Camping or staying overnight in Austrian forest requires permission from the owner. Outside the forest, the rules differ per state and municipality; a high-alpine emergency or short bivouac is not a national free pass.

Stay
Only with permission or according to local regulations
Cost
Varies
Water
Not guaranteed

Please check: Don't just pitch a tent in a forest, nature reserve or landscape protection area. Find an explicit trekking spot, hut or ask the municipality and landowner.

Austrian Forstgesetz §33 ↗
Italian bivacco

Alps and Apennines · Italy

Italy mainly regulates camping regionally, provincially, municipally and per park. A temporary alpine bivouac may be treated differently from recreational camping, but is not automatically permitted.

Stay
Only according to regional or park rules
Cost
Free where bivouac is explicitly permitted
Water
Not guaranteed

Please check: Check with the municipality or park manager before arrival. Only use unmanned bivacco huts according to their own rules and do not take any risks with fire in fire-prone areas.
